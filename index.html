The Basics
What MoRo is. What it can do. What it cannot.

MoRo is an Autonomous Mobile Robot developed by DiFacto Robotics & Automation. It's a machine that can independently navigate a factory floor, pick up materials, transport them, and deliver them — all without a human driver.

Three Words Worth Understanding

Autonomous means it makes its own decisions about where to go. No one pushes it, steers it, or directs it in real time. It figures out its path, avoids obstacles, and adapts when things change.

Mobile means it can move to different places. Unlike robotic arms fixed to a single workstation, MoRo travels across the factory floor — between machines, across departments, floor to floor.

Robot means it follows programmed logic. It doesn't think the way humans do. It operates within defined rules, executes assigned missions, and alerts humans when it encounters something outside its programming.

What MoRo Can Do
  • Navigate autonomously — No driver. It determines and follows its own path.
  • Transport loads reliably — Within its rated capacity, it delivers consistently.
  • Coordinate with machines — Works with conveyors, CNC lathes, lifts, and assembly cells.
  • Operate continuously — No breaks, no shift changes. 24/7 availability is standard.
  • Detect and avoid obstacles — Real-time sensing of people, equipment, and changes in environment.
  • Self-charge — Wireless charging at a dedicated dock. No cables to manage.
  • Report errors — Alerts operator immediately when something is outside its parameters.
What MoRo Cannot Do
  • Think creatively — It follows programmed logic, not intuition or judgment.
  • Handle unexpected situations alone — When something is outside its programming, it stops and waits for a human.
  • Exceed its payload limits — Each model has a rated maximum. Exceeding it risks damage.
  • Work in extreme environments — Designed for indoor factory conditions only.
"Understanding what MoRo cannot do is as important as understanding what it can. These boundaries are not weaknesses — they are safety features."

Three Variants
Same platform, three scales of capability.

DiFacto manufactures three versions of MoRo, each built for different factory demands. Despite their differences in size and payload capacity, all three share the same navigation technology, safety architecture, and software ecosystem. What you learn on one transfers completely to the others.

MoRo A-250 — Light Duty

Payload: 250 kg  |  Speed: 1.5 m/s  |  Weight: 160 kg

Designed for light assembly, small-part logistics, and environments where speed matters more than payload. Common in electronics, food & beverage, and pharmaceutical manufacturing.

MoRo A-500 — General Purpose (Most Common)

Payload: 500 kg  |  Speed: 1.2 m/s  |  Weight: 230 kg

The workhorse of the A-Series. Most customer installations use the A-500 as their primary unit. Ideal for machine tending, part transfer, and mid-weight manufacturing operations.

MoRo A-1200 — Heavy Duty

Payload: 1,200 kg  |  Speed: 0.8 m/s  |  Weight: 480 kg

Built for heavy material transfer, forklift automation, and high-load logistics in demanding industrial environments. Larger footprint, slower speed, built for strength.

How Navigation Works
No tape. No magnets. Just lasers and intelligence.

The most common question people ask when they first see MoRo: How does it know where it is without any markings on the floor?

The answer is a technology called ANT — Autonomous Navigation Technology — developed by BlueBotics and deployed in MoRo across all three variants. ANT uses a sensor called LiDAR to create and continuously update a precise map of the factory environment.

What LiDAR Does

LiDAR stands for Light Detection and Ranging. The sensor shoots invisible laser light in 360 degrees and measures how long it takes for each beam to bounce back from surrounding surfaces. From thousands of those measurements per second, MoRo builds a detailed picture of everything around it — walls, machines, pillars, people.

Simple Analogy

Imagine walking through a dark room and tapping a cane on every surface. The echoes tell you where the walls are. LiDAR does the same thing — but with laser light, at the speed of light, thousands of times per second. MoRo doesn't need to see in the human sense. It measures.

SLAM — The Intelligence Behind Navigation

SLAM stands for Simultaneous Localization and Mapping. It's the process that lets MoRo answer two questions at once, continuously: Where am I? and What's around me?

On its first pass through a new space, MoRo builds a map. On every subsequent pass, it compares what it currently sees against that stored map to confirm its precise location. Both happen at the same time — the map informs the position, the position refines the map.

A Practical Example
Machine Tending — Station A

The task: A CNC lathe at Station A needs parts loaded every 5 minutes. The parts bin is at Station B, 20 meters away.

With manual labor: 5 minutes per cycle — travel, load, return, repeat.

With MoRo: 50 seconds per cycle. Navigate to B (15s), wait for load (10s), navigate to A (15s), deliver (10s). That's 6 times faster on a single task. Over one 10-hour shift, one MoRo replaces the equivalent of several hours of repetitive human labor.

Safety on the Floor
Your responsibility. MoRo's limits. How they fit together.

MoRo is a capable machine, but it is still a machine. Working alongside it safely requires understanding how it behaves, where its limits are, and what your role is when something unexpected happens.

The Three Safety Zones
Safe Zone — 5 Metres and Beyond

MoRo moves at full operational speed in this range. Keep clear when it's in motion. If you enter this zone while MoRo is approaching, it will detect you and stop.

Caution Zone — 2 to 5 Metres

MoRo slows down in this range. You can work here, but stay aware. MoRo gives priority to you — it will stop or reroute rather than continue through.

Interaction Zone — 0 to 2 Metres

MoRo has stopped. It's waiting for a human interaction — load, unload, or manual override. You are in full control of this exchange.

What To Do When Things Go Wrong
  • MoRo is in your path: It detects you and stops. You can wait, or move aside. Either way, you are safe.
  • MoRo stops unexpectedly: Do not touch it. Do not push it. Call the operator or supervisor.
  • You hear an alarm: Stop. Move at least 2 metres away. Alert your supervisor immediately.
  • Battery warning appears: MoRo gives a light and sound warning, then drives itself to the charging dock.
  • A fault code appears on the display: Note the code and call the operator. Do not attempt to clear it yourself.
Your Responsibilities
You Are Responsible For
  • Keeping clear of MoRo's path when it's in motion
  • Keeping the floor free of debris, cables, and small objects
  • Reporting unusual behavior to your supervisor promptly
  • Respecting marked zones and warning indicators
You Must Never
  • Attempt to move or push MoRo manually
  • Touch its sensors, cables, or charging contacts
  • Block the charging dock
  • Exceed its rated payload capacity
  • Ignore alarm sounds or warning lights
"Respect MoRo as you would any piece of heavy industrial equipment. Its safety systems are excellent — but they work best when the humans around it are paying attention too."
